# References

Grouped by what each informed. Lunar sources are used where Mars data do not exist, and are marked.

## Human perception and navigation on foot
- Slopes along Apollo EVAs and slope misjudgment, EPSC 2024-196. Apollo 16 crew judged a crater wall as steeper than it was; low sun exaggerates slope. *(Lunar)*
- Oravetz, *Human estimation of slope, distance and height*, MIT thesis. https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/47809 *(Lunar; slope estimates larger at low sun)*
- Apollo 14 mission summary, Apollo Lunar Surface Journal. https://history.nasa.gov/alsj/a14/a14.summary.html *(Cone Crater rim not recognized)*
- Gramling et al., early Artemis surface navigation. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230012314 *(crews can be unsure of location; map frame consistency)*
- Lunar surface navigation survey. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250000720 *(Apollo navigation by eye and map)*

## Traverse planning and EVA tools
- Pena-Asensio et al., automated astronaut traverses with minimum metabolic workload, Acta Astronautica (2023). *(slope limit and hazard inputs)*
- NASA Artemis lunar surface EVA concept of operations. *(planning figures: 2 km/h, 20 degree slope, 2 km range)*
- SEXTANT, Surface Exploration Traverse Analysis and Navigation Tool, MIT. https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/119309 *(existing traverse planner; plans geometry, not visibility)*
- BASALT field analog program (Mars EVA science operations with communication delay). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6442304/
- NEREUS adaptive EVA route guidance, SpaceCHI 2025. *(current route tools rely on static, pre-mapped routes)*

## Display and human factors
- Joint Augmented Reality trade study, NASA. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230010234 *(display must be modular; nothing mounted to the helmet)*
- NASA-STD-3001 and NASA suit human-factors briefs. *(helmet field of regard must remain unencumbered)* This is why Vantage is designed for a wrist or tablet display.

## Mars terrain and data
- Curiosity boxwork campaign, NASA/JPL. https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-curiosity-rover-sees-martian-spiderwebs-up-close/ *(ridges about 1 to 2 m tall, sandy hollows; campaign from sol 4600)*
- Stack et al. (2022), J. Geophys. Res. Planets, doi:10.1029/2021JE007096 *(Gale terrain mapping)*
- Nachon et al. (2020), Mastcam viewsheds built from public data. *(method precedent for rover-image viewsheds)*
- USGS Astrogeology, MSL Gale DEM mosaic (Calef and Parker). https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/
- NASA MMGIS rover layers. https://mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/MSL/

## Hazards outside this tool's scope (for context)
- MSL Radiation Assessment Detector terrain-shielding results (Butte M12): measured reduction was about 5 percent, so terrain is not a radiation refuge.
- Norcross et al., NASA/TP-2009-214796, suited walkback test. *(metabolic cost of suited walking; treadmill data)*
